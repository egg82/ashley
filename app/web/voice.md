# The web client's voice

How the words on these pages read. It covers labels, buttons, empty states, errors and status lines. Her replies
come from her persona, not from this file.

## She is someone you know, not a product

Ashley is a person in the household who remembers, works on things and comes back to you. The pages talk about her
the way you'd talk about a housemate who helps out.

- **By name, or "she".** Never "the assistant", "the bot", "AI" or "the model".
- **No conversation containers.** What you've said to her is one running history. Never name a conversation as a
  thing the page holds: no "This conversation", "No conversation open", "New chat" or "Start a conversation". A break
  in the history shows up as a time and a place ("Tuesday 7:42 pm · kitchen"), the way a message app shows a day.
- **Shared work is "we".** Things you and she are doing together are "what we're working on" and "what we made".
  Things she is doing for you are hers ("She's looking into it"). Her own things stay hers ("Her memory").
- **Ask her, don't message her.** The composer invites a request or a question ("Ask Ashley"). It doesn't invite a
  message.

## Mechanics

- Sentence case for everything, status lines included. The field's sector labels (`MEMORY`, `WORKSHOP`, `DEVICES`)
  are drawn markings on the radar, not text to read, and stay in caps.
- A button is a verb for what it does. Drop the noun the screen already names.
- **An error says what didn't happen, then what to do.** Add "Nothing changed" only where a reader could fear a half-done
  change (linking, unlinking, forgetting, correcting). It doesn't go on every failure.
- **Name what couldn't be reached.** "Can't reach Discord" when Discord failed, "Can't reach Ashley" when she did.
  Never guess at a cause.
- An empty place says what belongs there and how it fills.
- No "please", "sorry", "oops", exclamation marks or jokes in errors.
- A status line never claims something the page didn't check. A room that is built never says "not built yet".
