Source: a CAD export 2.4 and a slicer, metric profile, in /home/person/work/birdhouse-1.2 (full write-up in FINDINGS.md there). Checked against piece 3's assumptions.

**Confirmed**
- Board cut: kerf <= 0 && boardCount % (width*3+1) == 0, offset trim(3) - 1. All 10 boards cut or none do; boards that don't cut take no trims.
- The shop list is private and used on only three lines (737, 794, 851). Roof, floor, perch and hinge take no trims.
- trim uses the u - r + m < 0 carry on 31-bit lengths.

**Corrections / gaps for piece 3**
- **Notches cut first at short lengths.** When ceil(length) <= 4, each notch takes one trim(2), and the number of notches is ceil((base + ceil(extra)) / 2), so 12 with an extra board. Each cut moves the list one step, so at short lengths the board cuts come later in the sequence.
- **Boards cut right to left**: cut 0 is the rightmost board. Unclear whether the spreadsheet maps them that way (the session had no spreadsheet source).
- **The roof isn't always there.** No roof on a model that shows its own lid (e.g. a feeder), and none of this runs in the flat-pack variant.
- **Carry shortcut must use >=.** Comparing against 1 << 31 matches the saw's < 0 only as >= 2**31. For width 3 a carry happens about 1 in 2^30.

**What a jump means**
The counter is boards since the shop opened. It doesn't reset on a new plan and stops while the saw is idle. A jump means a cut, a pause, a trip to the shelf, or a delay of more than 10 boards, not necessarily an edit.

- The maker's own cut list (still not found).
